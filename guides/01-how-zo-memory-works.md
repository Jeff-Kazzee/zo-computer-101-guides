Zo can remember you after a conversation ends, which means a new chat does not always require another round of introductions. Free gives you four hours of memory. Every paid plan includes persistent memory.

That last part is a huge deal.

A chat without persistent memory needs the background again. You explain the project, name the people involved, correct the tone, and repeat the same briefing when you start over. Persistent memory gives Zo continuity. Useful context can carry forward instead of disappearing with the conversation.

## The simple version

| Plan | Memory |
| --- | --- |
| Free | Zo remembers you for four hours |
| Basic, Pro, and Ultra | Zo has persistent memory across sessions |

Persistent means Zo's memory can continue when one session ends and another begins. You can leave, come back later, open a new conversation, and Zo may already have useful background from your earlier work.

It is closer to working with someone who knows you than opening a blank chatbot every time.

## What persistent memory feels like

Suppose you tell Zo about a bakery called Morning Crumb. It opens at 7 a.m. Jordan approves menu changes. Customer emails should sound warm and direct.

A few days later, you open a new conversation and ask:

> Write the Morning Crumb launch email for Jordan to review.

With persistent memory, Zo may already understand what Morning Crumb is, who Jordan is, and how the email should sound. You can get to the actual work without pasting the same briefing again.

Zo can carry useful background such as:

- Your preferences
- Project names and current work
- People you mention
- Corrections you have made
- Details that help a new request make sense

You still talk to Zo normally. Memory works in the background.

## Why this is one of Zo's best features

An AI assistant becomes far more useful when the relationship survives the chat window.

Persistent memory can make a fresh conversation feel connected to the work that came before it. Zo may recognize a project name without another paragraph of explanation. It may remember that you want short answers, that a product was renamed, or that one person handles approvals.

Small details add up. Ten minutes saved repeating context does not sound dramatic. Repeating that context across every project, every new chat, and every week gets old fast.

Zo's memory turns those separate conversations into an ongoing working relationship. That is a real advantage over treating each chat like a first meeting.

## Free gives you a taste of it

Free is the limited version of Zo. It gives you four hours of memory, enough to experience what it feels like when useful context can follow you into another conversation.

Basic, Pro, and Ultra all include persistent memory. You do not need the highest tier to get it. Moving to any paid plan turns that short experience into an ongoing part of using Zo.

## Memory can still need a correction

Zo may still miss a detail or remember something that has changed. Correct it when that happens.

Reopen the saved conversation when you need the original discussion. Put exact information in a file when you need a record you can inspect and edit.

The separate guides on files, `AGENTS.md`, and Rules cover those controls. This guide is about the built-in memory that follows you between conversations.

## What to tell Zo first

You do not need to configure a memory system. Start a conversation and give Zo the background that will make future work easier:

- What you want Zo to call you
- What you are working on
- How you prefer answers to be written
- The project names and people that will come up often

Then work normally. Correct what Zo gets wrong and refer to projects by name when you return.

On a paid plan, persistent memory can carry useful parts of that relationship forward. A new conversation may already know the names, preferences, and projects that would otherwise need another introduction.

You spend less time introducing your work to your own computer.
