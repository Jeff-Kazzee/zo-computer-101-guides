Your first hour in Zo should not be a tour. It should be a setup. The goal is to leave the room with a map, one connected app, one small thing you made, and one boundary so the next session is easier than the first. If you leave with a blank workspace and no connections, you just paid for confusion.

> The first hour is where you save yourself from future confusion. Spend it once.

## 1. Read the map before you touch anything

Open the workspace and find the root `AGENTS.md`. Read it. If you are going to touch a project, read that project's `README.md` and any local `AGENTS.md` too.

This is the part beginners skip and then spend a week rediscovering context one chat at a time. The map tells Zo what lives where. It also tells you what not to break.

If you are coming back to Zo after a while, this step is even more important. The workspace is your memory. Treat it like one.

## 2. Connect one app you already use every day

Pick the app that gives Zo immediate leverage. For most people that means Gmail or Google Calendar. If you live in Notion, connect Notion. If your work is in GitHub, connect GitHub.

Do not connect six things because you can. Connect one thing because you will use it tomorrow.

The fastest way to learn the platform is to ask Zo to do one real task through a connected app. Read an email thread. Pull a calendar event. Create a draft task. Something concrete. The first time I asked it to pull a real Gmail thread, the whole thing stopped feeling theoretical.

## 3. Open the browser and sign into one site that matters

Zo's browser is not decoration. It is how Zo handles the services that do not have a clean integration.

Open it, sign into one site you actually care about, and make sure the session sticks. This could be a dashboard, a vendor portal, a niche tool, anything that lives behind a login. If it fails, you want the failure to be boring, not a password manager meltdown. Do not make your first browser session your bank. That is a different conversation.

The point here is to prove to yourself that Zo can act as you when a browser session is the right tool.

## 4. Make one small thing on purpose

Create a tiny Space page, a short note, or a blank site just to see the thing exist.

I like small proof, because it keeps the first hour honest. A tool that can only be talked about is still a tool in theory. A tool that can make one thing is real.

You do not need to build a product. You need to make the first artifact.

## 5. Set one boundary now, not later

Choose one preference that Zo should not make you repeat.

Maybe it is a Rule about your writing voice. Maybe it is a Persona for reading-only work. Maybe it is a reminder that drafts should stay drafts until you say otherwise. Whatever it is, turn it into a stored instruction instead of a comment you keep retyping. A small boundary here saves a lot of stupid later.

This is where Zo starts to feel less like a chat and more like a system you are shaping.

## The minimum viable first hour

If you are short on time, do these three and stop:

1. Read the map.
2. Connect one app.
3. Make one small thing.

Everything else can wait.

The first hour is not supposed to impress anybody. It is supposed to make the second hour better.
