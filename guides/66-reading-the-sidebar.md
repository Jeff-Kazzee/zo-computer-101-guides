The first time you open Zo, the left sidebar throws a dozen names at you and most of them don't explain themselves. Zoa? Skills? Hosting and Space and Services, which sound like three words for the same thing. You can ignore that anxiety, because the truth is you'll live in about four of these on day one and the rest are there for when you need them.

> A sidebar full of doors looks like homework. It's actually a building you'll only ever use a few rooms of.

## The full map

Here's every destination, what it's for, and the honest answer to when you'll touch it.

| Destination | What it's for | When you'll use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Workspace | Your files and your chat with Zo, in the same place. The home base. | Constantly. This is where you actually work. |
| Terminal | A real command line on your Zo server, opens in a bottom panel. | When you want to run commands yourself instead of asking Zo to. |
| Search | Full-text search across your workspace files. | When the file you need is somewhere in a folder you've forgotten. |
| Zoa (Apps) | View and share the Zo Apps you've built. | Once you've made something worth sharing. |
| Automations | Scheduled tasks that run on their own, like a morning brief. | When you want Zo doing work while you sleep. |
| Hosting / Sites | Manage your Sites and Services. Your Space lives here too. | When you publish a website or run a long-lived service. |
| Skills | Repeatable AI workflows you save and reuse, with a community Catalog. | When you find yourself explaining the same multi-step job twice. |
| Integrations | Connect Gmail, Calendar, Notion, and a thousand-plus apps via OAuth or MCP. | When you want Zo to read or write in your other tools. |
| Browser | Zo's own browser. Log into a site so Zo can see it. | When Zo needs a page that's behind a login. |
| Settings | AI models, personas, rules, providers, channels, tools, and your secrets. | When you're tuning how Zo behaves, not what it's doing. |
| Billing / Account | Plan, credits, usage, invoices, payment. | When you want to know what you're spending and on what. |
| Fan Club | The community side of things. | When you feel like company. |

A few of these are not what their name suggests. Space, Sites, and Services are related but not the same thing. Space is the managed personal site you get by default, Sites are full projects you own as folders, and Services are long-running processes. And the settings that matter most to power users are buried one level down.

## Where the good stuff hides under Settings

Settings looks like a junk drawer until you open it. Under Settings, in the AI section, are the four knobs that change how Zo thinks:

- **Models** picks which AI model drives your chat.
- **Personas** let you give Zo a defined role and voice, so it answers like a specific kind of collaborator instead of a generic assistant.
- **Rules** are standing instructions Zo follows every time, like "never use em dashes" or "always plan before building."
- **Providers / BYOK** is where you bring your own API keys if you'd rather run a model through your own account.

If you ever read "set up a persona" or "add a rule" in another guide and went looking for a top-level tab, this is why you couldn't find it. They're under Settings, not out front.

## The ones you'll actually live in

Day one, you need four, and the marketing will not tell you which.

**Workspace.** This is 90 percent of your time. Files on the left, chat with Zo on the right, both looking at the same thing. If you only ever learn one part of Zo, learn this one.

**Terminal.** You will reach for this faster than you expect, because sometimes typing `ls` yourself beats asking. It's a normal Linux shell with root, sitting one panel-tap away.

**Settings.** Not glamorous, but it's where you stop fighting the defaults. One good rule saved here ends a category of repeated corrections.

**Integrations or Hosting**, depending on who you are. If you're connecting Zo to your email and calendar, you live in Integrations. If you're shipping a site or a service, it's Hosting. Pick the one that matches what you're actually here to do, and let the other wait.

Everything else, Automations, Skills, Browser, Zoa, you'll discover the day you have a reason to. That day is a good day. It means you've outgrown asking Zo to do a thing by hand and you're ready to make it automatic.
